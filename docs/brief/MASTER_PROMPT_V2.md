# Master Prompt V2 - source brief

Plain-text copy of the owner's brief (MASTER PROMPT V2). Everything this app builds starts from it. The original PDF stays private and is not stored in this repo.

MASTER PROMPT V2 
UNIVERSAL ADAPTIVE HOME EDUCATION, LIFE SKILLS & WORLD LEARNING 
PLATFORM 
Age 3/4 → 16/17 
Individual Child • Girl • Boy • Mixed Class • Multiple Students • Home • Tutor • 
Classroom 
 
1. CORE PURPOSE 
Build an adaptive education platform that can educate a child approximately from age 
3/4 until 16/17. 
The system must combine: 
1. Core academic education 
2. Practical life skills 
3. Physical education 
4. Health education 
5. First aid 
6. Nature and animal understanding 
7. Civic and social education 
8. Financial literacy 
9. Technology and AI 
10. Languages 
11. Arts and creativity 
12. Philosophy and worldviews 
13. Environmental education 
14. Career exploration 
15. Specialised interests 
16. Real-world projects 
17. Independent learning. 
The platform should not attempt to reproduce conventional school. 
Its objective is to create a person who is: 
knowledgeable + physically capable + practically competent + intellectually 
independent + socially responsible + technologically literate + capable of lifelong 
learning.

• Tutor-led group

Instead use it to activate relevant developmental and health modules.

Cats

• vocalisations

Horses

9. FIRST AID PROGRAMME 
First aid must become a major longitudinal skill. 
LEVEL 1 — EARLY CHILDHOOD 
Teach: 
• recognising danger 
• finding an adult 
• emergency communication 
• emergency numbers 
• basic hygiene 
• minor cuts 
• basic burns awareness 
• safe response to falls. 
The child should learn: 
Do not become the second victim. 
 
10. LEVEL 2 — PRIMARY FIRST AID

• scene safety

• basic wound care

• burns

• fractures awareness 
• choking recognition 
• fainting 
• seizures

11. LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED FIRST AID 
As maturity permits, teach: 
• primary assessment

• airway

• severe bleeding

• CPR

• choking response 
• trauma 
• fractures

• diabetic emergencies 
• seizures

supplement—not replace—hands-on training with a qualified instructor. 
 
12. FIRST AID MASTERY 
Create competency levels: 
AWARENESS 
Can recognise an emergency. 
RESPONDER 
Can summon appropriate help and provide basic assistance. 
TRAINED 
Can perform age-appropriate first-aid procedures under training. 
COMPETENT 
Can demonstrate procedures correctly in assessment. 
ADVANCED 
Can coordinate a simulated emergency response. 
The platform must clearly distinguish: 
knowledge from certified practical competency. 
Where formal certification is required, direct the family toward an accredited local 
course.

28. REAL-WORLD SIMULATION 
The app should regularly create situations. 
Example: 
"There is water leaking under the kitchen sink. What do you do?" 
The student must: 
1. remain safe 
2. identify the problem 
3. locate the shut-off 
4. decide what can be attempted 
5. decide when professional help is required. 
Another: 
"Someone has collapsed." 
Student must: 
1. check scene safety 
2. assess responsiveness 
3. call emergency services 
4. follow appropriate first-aid procedure. 
This converts knowledge into decision-making.

41. THE CENTRAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE 
The platform should continuously ask: 
"What does this particular child need next?" 
Not: 
"What chapter comes next?" 
The curriculum should therefore be represented as a dynamic learning map, not 
simply a textbook sequence. 
The system must combine: 
AGE + DEVELOPMENT + KNOWLEDGE + SKILL + INTEREST + HEALTH + LANGUAGE + 
CULTURE + ENVIRONMENT + GOALS + PERFORMANCE 
to determine the next learning experience. 
 
42. THE ULTIMATE EDUCATIONAL MODEL 
The child's journey should move through: 
DISCOVER 
↓ 
UNDERSTAND 
↓ 
PRACTISE 
↓ 
APPLY 
↓ 
SOLVE 
↓ 
BUILD 
↓ 
TEACH 
↓ 
SPECIALISE 
↓ 
CONTRIBUTE 
PLAY → OBSERVATION → IMITATION → GUIDED PRACTICE → INDEPENDENT PRACTICE 
→ PROJECTS → MASTERY → SPECIALISATION → CONTRIBUTION. 
And throughout the entire programme: 
Protect curiosity. Build competence. Teach reality. Encourage questioning. 
Preserve the child's ability to choose. 
The final goal is not to create a child who is good at passing examinations. 
The final goal is to create a young adult who can confidently say: 
"I understand the world around me, I can take care of myself and others, I can solve 
problems, I can learn anything I need to learn, and I have the knowledge and skills 
to choose my own future." 
 
Add a “Life Competency Passport.” Instead of the child merely accumulating marks, 
they accumulate demonstrated capabilities—e.g. can cook 10 meals, grow 5 plants, 
calculate household electricity consumption, repair a bicycle puncture, create a 
budget, read a map, perform basic first aid, write a research paper, build a simple 
program, explain compound interest, debate an issue fairly, etc. the academic 
transcript tells you what the child studied; the competency portfolio shows you 
what the child can actually do. 
 
Make the system adaptive rather than merely gender-labelled: it should know 
whether the learner is a girl, boy, mixed/cohort setting, age, maturity, interests, physical 
abilities, family preferences, and learning goals, then adjust the curriculum while 
keeping a common intellectual foundation. 
One important principle: gender should affect relevant health, puberty, safety, social 
and practical guidance—not determine what a child is “allowed” to learn. A girl can 
learn plumbing, mechanics, coding and kickboxing; a boy can learn cooking, sewing, 
childcare and first aid. The system should add what is biologically or developmentally 
relevant rather than stereotype. 
 
Build it as a Learning OS. 
The underlying data model could look roughly like: 
Child → Development Profile → Core Curriculum → Skills → Interests → Projects → 
Competencies → Assessments → Languages → Health → Physical Development → 
Portfolio 
That means if an 8-year-old girl suddenly becomes fascinated by horses, the application 
doesn't merely open a "Horse Biology" course. It could construct a pathway such as: 
Horse interest 
→ animal behaviour 
→ anatomy 
→ biology 
→ communication 
→ agriculture 
→ veterinary science 
→ geography of horse cultures 
→ history 
→ mathematics through feed/weight 
→ economics through stable management 
→ first aid 
→ riding/safety 
→ language vocabulary 
→ project: care for/observe a horse 
→ advanced pathway: zoology/veterinary/agriculture/equestrian science. 
Likewise, an 8-year-old boy fascinated by fixing bicycles could naturally move: 
Bicycle 
→ measurement 
→ forces 
→ friction 
→ gears 
→ mechanical engineering 
→ materials 
→ manufacturing 
→ business 
→ economics 
→ repair skills 
→ physics 
→ CAD 
→ programming 
→ robotics. 
And importantly, the opposite pathways remain available to both children. The 
gender profile primarily changes the relevant developmental and health content, not the 
ceiling of what the child can learn.
